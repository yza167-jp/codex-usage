# v1.8.0 — current-epoch quota calibration

## Why this release exists

v1.7.3 could select two old, complete one-percentage-point intervals forever,
while discarding every new interval containing an unresolved service tier. Its
weighted median then continued to divide current reference credits by an old
scale even when the local-week estimate exceeded the same-period backend total.
Old overlapping plateau intervals could also overstate the amount of evidence.

This release changes the **estimator policy**, not model prices or token counting.
There is no new fixed 550-credits conversion, Astra 1.10 factor, account-specific
pricing constant, cap at 100%, or forced normalization to the backend total.
Issue #5 is outside this release's scope.

## Current-epoch replay

Raw backend snapshots and indexed token deltas are the inputs. On each query:

1. Select snapshots for the account, plan, reset timestamp, window duration,
   limit ID and explicit Unknown-tier pricing mode, at or before the snapshot
   being reported. Legacy snapshots without a limit ID are imported only for
   the default Codex pool; their original pool identity cannot be recovered.
2. Starting from the earliest snapshot in the current regime, freeze a sample
   at the **first snapshot showing at least 2 percentage points of growth**.
   Repeated readings on the same plateau cannot add independent votes. This is
   a noise-reduction threshold, not an official quota-meter resolution.
3. Use exactly shared endpoints and count local events in **(start, end]**.
   The next sample begins at the previous endpoint; a boundary token is counted
   once. Excluded samples advance the endpoint too.
4. Re-price already indexed events under the unchanged reference rate card and
   classify each sample: `complete`, `assumed_tier`, or `excluded`.
5. Prefer current samples. Combine accepted samples as **sum(local credits) /
   sum(percentage-point growth)**. With at least three samples, an isolated
   ratio outside 0.5–2.0 times their median is excluded. This heuristic cannot
   prove that a particular interval contains external usage.

A decrease in used percentage starts a new regime even if resets_at is unchanged;
no sample bridges it. Earlier samples in that regime are discarded from the
rebuildable derived table, not from the raw audit trail. Intervals ending at
100% are censored and cannot teach a rate: later work may not be included usage.
Malformed relevant histories, unsupported providers, Flex, unpriced models and
Fast without a known multiplier are excluded from learning. Zero-local-credit
quota movements cannot establish a conversion.

## Unknown is not unpriced

A known model without a persisted tier uses the tool's explicit existing
assumption: Standard by default, or Fast for Unknown segments with `--fast`.
Both the sample numerator and per-session display use that same assumption.
Such samples may update the scale but stay **LOW confidence**, however many
queries are made. They are not promoted to complete token/price evidence.
The header separates `complete` and `assumed` sample counts. Unknown-price models
such as Spark are different: their intervals are excluded until priceable.

## Fallback and validation

Current samples win. Without them, a usable current cumulative baseline can
provide a **LOW, reconciliation** estimate once used percentage is at least 3%
and below 100%. Since it is fitted to the current total, agreement with that
total is not independent validation, and baseline coverage is not reported.

A new-policy scale from a prior epoch is at most a **LOW historical prior**, with
sample age limited to seven days relative to the reported snapshot. It does not
count as current evidence. When none is usable, the existing labeled plan `SEED`
keeps estimates available. Legacy scalar interval conversions are not silently
imported into the new policy; raw snapshots are replayed instead.

`MEDIUM` requires at least three complete accepted samples, 8pp of non-overlapping
movement and moderate dispersion. `HIGH` requires at least five, 20pp and lower
dispersion. These labels describe observational support, not provider-verified
billing accuracy. A known assumption always caps confidence at LOW.

When local-week usage at the matching snapshot exceeds backend usage by more than
max(3pp, 25% of backend used), **CALIBRATION MISMATCH** is shown. No values are
clipped or silently reconciled. A changed regime or unsupported pool has its own
warning. Snapshots older than 30 minutes are explicitly marked stale.

## Output semantics

- `CURRENT WEEK` remains the last-observed backend percentage with its timestamp.
- `WEEKLY≈` is selected local reference-credit usage divided by the estimated
  scale. It is not the exact debit of a subscription allowance.
- `~` marks assumed/partial weekly values, replacing `≥`. An uncertain numerator
  **and** learned denominator do not establish a mathematical quota lower bound.
- `CREDITS* +` still means incomplete reference pricing. Raw credits, tokens,
  cached input, reasoning treatment, model order and session/agent rollup are
  unchanged. Cross-reset reports remain a current-scale weekly equivalent, not
  the actual sum of debits from historical allowance windows.
- JSON and CSV retain `weekly_estimate_is_lower_bound` for compatibility but set
  it to false/0. `weekly_estimate_is_partial` and policy/sample/warning metadata
  expose the distinction. Consumers relying on the old flag must update.
- The JSON coverage field is retained, while the terminal says `local/backend≈`;
  this is a consistency ratio, not proof of device-level quota attribution.

Other devices/shared usage, lag or rounding in snapshots, missing or duplicated
local history, and model/tier mix changes can still bias estimates. This patch
fixes stale/overlapping calibration, **not** every possible token-lineage issue.
A total that fits the backend does not independently validate the raw logs.

## Migration and performance

Token cache schema remains **3**. Reference-rate revision is unchanged. The new
policy is `2026-09-11-v180-epoch-replay`; two additive tables,
`quota_snapshots_v180` and `quota_samples_v180`, isolate scoped snapshots and
derived samples. Original observations, intervals, snapshots, token events and
file index rows are retained. Do not delete or rebuild the cache to upgrade.

Current-week deltas are sorted and prefix-summed once per query. Intervals then
use binary searches, not repeated transcript scans or full session traversals.
The derived current-epoch samples are safely rebuilt; repeated queries do not
create duplicate evidence. This is local-only, with no new auth/network access.

## Validation and sources

Synthetic fixtures reproduce the reported 264.253 stale-scale failure and use
fresh model/tier events for replay. Representative interval aggregates yield
about 549.5 credits/pp and 56.3% for 30,937.1 credits, rather than 117.1%. These
are fixture results, **not** a validation of the user's real transcript history.
The fixture's current-week result is not forced to equal its backend total.
Tests also cover migration, repeated queries, cache/no-cache parity, scopes,
resets, saturation, assumptions, exclusions, exports and terminal warnings.

OpenAI's [app-server protocol](https://developers.openai.com/codex/app-server/)
describes usedPercent, windowDurationMins and resetsAt. It does not publish a
fixed Plus/Pro credits-to-weekly-percent conversion. This release adds no new
pricing claims; the source-pinned reference rate card is unchanged.
