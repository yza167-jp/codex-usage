# v1.8.0 — consolidated quota calibration and scoped migration

This release incorporates the concurrently merged v1.7.4 fix rather than
replacing it with a second estimator. It preserves that version's interval
policy, explicit reconciliation behavior, export fields and regression tests.
It adds complete snapshot/storage scope isolation and legacy-anchor replay.

## The user-facing repair (v1.7.3 → v1.8.0)

Two complete old 1pp intervals could keep the scale at 264.253 credits/pp while
all new intervals with Unknown tiers were ignored. Reference usage entirely
since reset could then display 117.1% while the backend total was only 62%.

Current-period snapshots and indexed token deltas now take precedence. Freeze a
sample at the first snapshot at least **3pp** above its anchor, and let that
endpoint become the next anchor. Tokens are counted on **(start, end]**. Queries
on the same plateau do not create independent samples or overlapping blocks.
Small final movements stay pending instead of receiving inflated confidence.

Known-model Unknown tiers may teach a **LOW, assumed-tier** estimate using the
same explicit credit basis as the report: Standard by default, Fast only for
Unknown with `--fast`. Unpriced models, Flex, missing/malformed parent history,
unsupported providers/tiers and Fast without a known multiplier are excluded;
later priceable intervals can still learn. More queries cannot make an
assumption into an observed service tier.

Current eligible blocks from the last 48h take priority over historical priors.
The scale is aggregate reference credits divided by independent percentage
movement, with outlier rejection only after enough blocks. Historical cohorts
remain dated LOW priors for at most 14 days. A visible plan SEED is the last
fallback. The reference-rate map, tier pricing, seeds and token accounting are
unchanged; there is no hardcoded 550 conversion or Astra 1.10 factor.

For complete evidence, MEDIUM requires at least 3 blocks/10pp; HIGH requires
6 blocks/20pp and max/min ratio no greater than 1.5. Any assumed tier keeps LOW.
These thresholds are tool heuristics, not provider rules or statistical bounds.

## Conflict and partial-result semantics

The local-current-period estimate is compared with the backend at the **same
snapshot timestamp**. Overestimation beyond max(3pp, 25% of backend usage)
produces a **CALIBRATION MISMATCH** warning. With eligible history, an explicitly
labeled local-only `reconciled_baseline` may be used, retaining the rejected
scale and warning. Otherwise the conflict remains visible. Values are not
clipped to 62% or 100%, and reconciliation is not independent validation.

A drop in percentage under the same reset ID starts a new evidence segment;
no sample crosses the drop and whole-period reconciliation is disabled.
Saturated endpoints do not teach a slope. Stale/expired snapshots are flagged.

Partial weekly numbers use **`56.3%?`** rather than `≥56.3%`; `?` marks a partial
estimate, not a mathematical lower bound. CREDITS* and its `+` marker retain
reference-pricing semantics. JSON/CSV keep the old lower-bound flag as false/0,
with separate partial and calibration provenance fields. `CURRENT WEEK` remains
the backend snapshot; `WEEKLY≈` is a local weekly-equivalent estimate. Cross-reset
reports can exceed 100% legitimately. `local_coverage_percent` stays null.

## Additional v1.8.0 hardening

v1.7.4 added `limit_id` to the raw snapshot table, but the old primary key still
omitted pool and window duration. Simultaneous different-pool observations could
therefore overwrite one another. The new `quota_snapshots_v180` primary key
includes account, plan, pricing mode, pool, reset, duration and timestamp.
New scoped rows win equal-time collisions during replay; old raw rows are kept.

Compatible raw `quota_snapshots` and `quota_observations` are imported as backend
anchors, not as scalar credit conversions. Old stored credit amounts and old
rate-card scales are never used for replay pricing. Observation-only records
without pool metadata are considered only for the default Codex pool. Their
original pool identity cannot be recovered; this legacy limitation is explicit.
Future or out-of-window anchors and other accounts/plans/modes/pools are excluded.

The derived `quota_intervals_v3` key also includes duration. Its policy revision
is `2026-09-11-window-v3-scoped` combined with the unchanged reference-rate
revision. v1.7.4's `quota_intervals_v2` is retained, not overwritten or deleted.
All v1.7.4 functionality/tests are preserved; model-specific pools cannot
calibrate the full-account workload and missing parents remain ineligible.

## Upgrade and performance

**Do not delete or rebuild the cache.** Schema-3 token events/files, old raw
quota snapshots/observations/intervals, and v1.7.4 derived rows remain intact.
Only the new current-policy derived rows are rebuilt. Upgrades work directly
from v1.7.3 or through v1.7.4. No new flag, auth access or network call is needed.

Token deltas are prefix-summed once per query. Interval queries use binary
searches on that ledger, not repeated JSONL scans. Warm-cache/no-cache consistency
and zero transcript rereads on repeat CLI queries are regression-tested.
PROJECT/SESSION separation, price-descending models, full model continuation
lines and the 144-cell report cap are unchanged.

## Validation and limits

134 tests pass locally, preserving all 118 v1.7.4 tests and adding 16 scope and
migration regressions. The retained audit-shaped fixture replaces the old
264.253 scale with about **541.0 credits/pp**, using 19pp of independent assumed
movement; its final 2pp movement is pending under the 3pp policy. Applying that
scale to 30,937.1 reference credits yields about **57.2%**, not 117.1%.

These are synthetic receipts with illustrative aggregate amounts, not the user's
actual transcripts. Exact real results depend on available raw anchors, token
history and new activity. Matching a backend total does not certify token
lineage/deduplication or device-only attribution. Other devices, shared usage,
rounding/lag and workload composition can bias estimates.

The primary [Codex app-server protocol](https://developers.openai.com/codex/app-server/)
describes usedPercent, windowDurationMins and resetsAt; it is not a fixed
Plus/Pro credits-to-weekly-percent rate card. Issue #5 and its discussion are
unchanged; no unverified pricing rule is introduced.
